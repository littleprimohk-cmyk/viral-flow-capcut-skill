# Retained viral-reference profile

## The benchmark

A roughly 55-second vertical personal-brand video tells a mother's story about her daughter learning to swim. The creator identified it as the account's strongest video for reach/shares and low drop-off. No analytics were provided. This package retains an editorial analysis, not the original child's images or video.

| Approx. time | Narrative job |
|---|---|
| 0–3s | A concrete day and an unexpected first remark create curiosity. |
| 3–7s | The child says learning was not to make her parents happy: reveal the conflict early. |
| 7–11s | A succinct viewpoint turns a family incident into a wider relationship question. |
| 11–20s | Several summers of lessons and wanting to quit the previous evening give the outcome weight. |
| 20–30s | A meal-time conversation shows the mother's initial interpretation and the child's correction. |
| 30–35s | The positive reason arrives: the child wanted to do something well for herself. |
| 35–48s | Parental expectations become the child's own motivation; the mother revises her understanding. |
| 48–55s | Return to the swimming story, now understood as a child developing her own ideas. |

## Reusable mechanism

**Specific lived event → unexpected remark/complete contradiction → early conflict → concrete history and obstacles → reconstructed conversation or experience → changed understanding → broader relational meaning → callback payoff.**

Share hypotheses: it gives the viewer a message to send to a partner, parent or friend; the creator reflects on herself before advising others; the conflict has a warm resolution; the final line works outside the original story. Retention hypotheses: immediate entry, partial answer followed by deeper curiosity, new information each beat and visuals that support the story. These are hypotheses, not algorithm claims.

## What transfers to a new video

Transfer narrative functions, not literal swimming content, a fixed 55-second duration, family footage, clothing, colours or every animation. In the current workflow, deliver flow-only cuts unless the user requests design.

For “Soft voice, wild truth” videos, the fixed signature and that recording's English thesis follow the new hook. The signature is a creator requirement; do not claim it was universally responsible for the benchmark's performance.

## Lessons from the comparison-video revision

1. Two early 40–50-second cuts removed important connective details. The creator rejected them as too short and incomplete. Keep the full journey when it matters.
2. “Every milestone upgrades my comparison target” names a phenomenon but does not finish the emotional point.
3. The accepted A hook expresses the full cycle: **I have progressed → I find someone further ahead → I feel inadequate again.**
4. A then returns to university, tells a real business achievement, shows comparison returning, explains current independent brand choices and ends by questioning someone else's timeline.
5. B opens with “Am I moving too slowly? Am I not good enough?”, develops social-media comparison, shows an achievement did not cure it, then reaches the insight and present-day change.
6. Do not repeat the full A hook again in the body. B can reserve the full insight for the later reveal.
7. The creator requires reviewing two complete written flows before CapCut editing, and specifically requested no added text.
8. Approval of one better hook led to a request to see both full flows again; it was not permission to start editing.

## Hook completeness check

Can a new viewer understand the situation, tension and emotional cost without reading a caption? Does the next section naturally explain or develop it? Can every spoken word be assembled from the source without distorting meaning? Prefer a complete 7–10-second contradiction over an obscure two-second fragment when the source calls for it; duration remains editorial judgment.
